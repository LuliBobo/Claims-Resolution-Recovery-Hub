# Claims Resolution & Recovery Hub — Demo Script Screen by Screen

> **Adapted note:** the original version of this script used a Spanish-language flagship complaint. The flagship case actually built in Luo (Elena Rostova, order #ORD-9842, DPD) is in English, so the flagship references below were updated to match. Screen 7's multilingual showcase now points to the Slovak-language wrong-item case (Martin Horvath) instead.

## Purpose of this script
This script is designed for a **3-minute to 5-minute hackathon demo**. Its purpose is to help present the product clearly, quickly and in the right order.

The main principle is simple:

> Do not demo features. Demo one transformation.

That transformation is:

> **A complaint about a broken vase becomes a customer replacement and a carrier recovery claim in under one minute.**

Everything on screen should support that story.

---

## Demo structure at a glance
The strongest flow is:

1. Problem
2. Inbox intake
3. Case understanding
4. Evidence check
5. Policy support
6. Resolution recommendation
7. Recovery draft
8. Human approval
9. Audit trail
10. Analytics / business value

Total recommended time: **3 minutes 30 seconds to 4 minutes 30 seconds**.

---

## Demo setup before presenting
Before the live demo starts, prepare the workspace so that:

- the dashboard loads without delay,
- one flagship case already exists,
- the damaged-vase case is easy to open,
- policy documents are already uploaded,
- order and shipment data are already linked,
- one recovery draft is ready to preview,
- analytics already contain at least one repeated issue pattern.

Do not waste demo time on typing long sample data unless typing itself is part of the wow moment.

## Slide 0 / Opening sentence
### What to say
> Small e-commerce teams still handle complaints through inboxes, spreadsheets and manual follow-up. We built Claims Resolution & Recovery Hub to turn one complaint into a customer resolution and a recovery action, with AI assistance and human control.

### What to show
Show the dashboard for 3 to 5 seconds.

### Goal
Set the problem and the promise immediately.

## Screen 1 — Dashboard
### What to show
Open the Dashboard view.

Visible elements should include:
- Open Cases
- Waiting for Evidence
- Waiting for Approval
- Recovery Opportunities
- Repeated Issue Patterns
- Recent Cases

### What to say
> This is the operations control room. Instead of searching inboxes and spreadsheets, the team sees which complaints are open, which ones are missing evidence, which ones need approval and where recovery money may be lost.

### Time
15 to 20 seconds.

### Important
Do not stay here too long. The dashboard is context, not the story.

---

## Screen 2 — Cases Inbox
### What to show
Open Cases Inbox and click the flagship case.

Case title example:
> Broken Vase — DPD Delivery — Carrier Damage Suspected

### What to say
> Here is our flagship case. A customer wrote that a glass vase arrived broken and uploaded photos. Normally this would trigger manual forwarding between support, operations and the warehouse.

### Time
15 to 20 seconds.

### Goal
Move quickly from abstract dashboard to a real case.

---

## Screen 3 — Case detail overview
### What to show
Inside the case detail page, show these fields first:
- Customer language
- Complaint text
- Internal English summary
- Case type
- Linked order
- Linked shipment

### What to say
> The system detects the language, creates an internal English summary and classifies the issue as damaged delivery. It also links the complaint to the original order, shipment and carrier context.

### Time
20 to 25 seconds.

### Goal
Make the AI value visible very early.

---

## Screen 4 — Attachments and evidence checker
### What to show
Scroll to the attachments and evidence checker panel.

Make visible:
- attached product photo,
- evidence checklist,
- missing outer packaging photo,
- evidence status.

### What to say
> The important part is not just understanding the complaint. The system checks whether the evidence is complete. Here it sees that the product photo exists, but the outer-box photo is still missing, which matters for a carrier recovery claim.

### Time
20 to 25 seconds.

### Goal
Show that the app is operational, not cosmetic.

---

## Screen 5 — Policy support
### What to show
Open the Policy Support section.

Display:
- rule name,
- policy document,
- version,
- short excerpt.

### What to say
> Recommendations are not generated as a black box. The app retrieves the relevant internal complaint and recovery rule, shows the supporting policy excerpt and makes the logic inspectable by the operator.

### Time
20 seconds.

### Goal
Build trust and reduce the “just another AI assistant” impression.

### What to show
Open the Resolution Proposal section.

Display:
- recommendation,
- rationale,
- confidence,
- customer impact,
- business exposure,
- needs human approval.

### What to say
> Based on the complaint type, evidence status and policy support, the system recommends sending a replacement. It also explains why, shows the confidence and marks whether human approval is required.

20 seconds.

### Goal
Show controlled decision support.

## Screen 7 — Multilingual customer reply draft
### What to show
Open the customer reply draft.

Make visible:
- English customer-facing draft (this case's language),
- English internal operator summary (identical here, since the complaint was already English).

### What to say
> The customer reply is generated in the customer's original language, while the operator always gets an internal English summary. For this case both happen to be English — the same module produces a Slovak draft on the Martin Horvath wrong-item case, worth a quick second click if there's time to show multilingual output.

### Time
20 seconds.

### Goal
Show international relevance and practical time saving.

---

## Screen 8 — Recovery draft
### What to show
Open the Recovery Draft panel or Recovery Queue.

Display:
- counterparty type,
- counterparty name,
- claim type,
- estimated recoverable value,
- linked evidence summary,
- status.

### What to say
> This is the differentiator. The same complaint does not stop at the customer response. The system also prepares a carrier recovery draft, so the merchant can try to recover the loss instead of only absorbing it.

### Time
20 to 25 seconds.

### Goal
This is one of the two strongest moments in the demo. Slow down slightly here.

---

## Screen 9 — Human review
### What to show
Open Review Queue or review actions within the case.

Show the available actions:
- Approve
- Approve with Edits
- Request More Evidence
- Reject

Then click Approve.

### What to say
> AI structures the case and prepares the action, but a human remains in control. Important customer and recovery actions are explicitly reviewed before anything moves forward.

### Time
15 to 20 seconds.

### Goal
Show accountability and reduce risk concerns.

---

## Screen 10 — Audit timeline
### What to show
Open the Audit Timeline.

Highlight the visible sequence:
- case created,
- language detected,
- classified,
- evidence checked,
- policy retrieved,
- recommendation generated,
- recovery draft created,
- human approval recorded.

### What to say
> Every important step is logged in an audit timeline. That matters because complaint handling is not just about speed. Teams also need consistency, accountability and a record of what happened.

### Time
15 to 20 seconds.

### Goal
Make the product feel mature and enterprise-capable even as an MVP.

---

## Screen 11 — Analytics / root-cause value
### What to show
Return to Analytics or a root-cause panel.

Highlight:
- repeated issue by SKU,
- repeated issue by carrier,
- recovery opportunities,
- complaint trend.

### What to say
> The long-term value is not only faster complaint handling. Over time, the system shows which products, suppliers or carriers create repeated losses, so the team can improve operations and reduce future claims.

### Time
15 to 20 seconds.

### Goal
End on business value, not only on workflow automation.

## Recommended full spoken flow
Use this if you want one continuous spoken version:

> Small e-commerce teams still handle complaints through inboxes, spreadsheets and manual follow-up. We built Claims Resolution & Recovery Hub to turn one complaint into a customer resolution and a recovery action, with AI assistance and human control. This is the operations dashboard, where the team can immediately see open cases, missing evidence, approval bottlenecks and recovery opportunities. Here is our flagship case: a complaint about a broken vase. The app detects the language, creates an internal English summary, classifies the issue as damaged delivery and links it to the original order and shipment. It then checks the evidence and notices that the product photo is present, but the outer-box photo is still missing. Instead of acting like a black box, it retrieves the relevant internal policy and shows the exact rule supporting the next step. Based on that, it recommends a replacement, drafts a customer reply and also prepares a carrier recovery draft so the merchant can recover the loss. A human reviewer approves the actions, and every step is recorded in the audit timeline. Over time, the analytics layer shows repeated issues by SKU, supplier or carrier. So the product does not just answer complaints faster — it helps teams recover money and reduce repeat operational failures.

## What to avoid during the demo
- Do not explain the full data model.
- Do not show too many sample cases.
- Do not spend time editing configuration screens.
- Do not oversell the legal side.
- Do not claim automatic external execution.
- Do not lead with AI terminology before the business problem is clear.
- Do not describe every module as if this were a product tour.

The audience should remember one thing:

> **This app takes a complaint and turns it into a controlled operational outcome.**

---

## Backup plan if live AI fails
If a live generation step becomes slow or inconsistent:

1. open the pre-generated case,
2. say “The system has already processed this complaint,”
3. walk through the outputs,
4. focus on the workflow chain rather than the live generation.

For hackathons, a stable demo is better than a risky fully live generation.
